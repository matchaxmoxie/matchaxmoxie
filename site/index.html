    Why clear next steps matter

    National U.S. numbers (not IU-specific). First-generation and low-income students often face steeper gaps early. Clear next steps and real mentors close what the brochure leaves blank.

    • 20% vs 42%

      Among 2002 U.S. high school sophomores who later enrolled in college, 20% of first-generation students had earned a bachelor’s degree by 2012, compared with 42% of students whose parent held a bachelor’s or higher. (Source)

    • 33% vs 14%

      Three years after first enrolling (2003 to 04 cohort), 33% of first-generation students had left postsecondary education without a credential, compared with 14% of peers whose parents held a bachelor’s. (Source)

    • 47% vs 58%

      Among students who entered four-year institutions in 2014 to 15, Pell Grant recipients had a 47% eight-year completion rate, compared with 58% for nonrecipients. (Source)

    Week one · month one · soft landings

    Campus week reminders

    Not a brochure. Not a vlog. Just the next honest moves when IU feels big. Verify halls, meal plans, and calendars on live IU pages for your term.

    1. Hectic is normal. First week feels long. Establish a schedule once classes start. Finishing Friday is a real win.
    2. Walk the route. Find your classrooms before day one. Leave buffer between buildings ... campus maps confuse everyone at first.
    3. Know the map. Central, north, northwest, south. Name the dining hall that feels calm for you.
    4. Map your week. Mark busy days vs free blocks. Use free time for homework so nights can be quieter.
    5. Nerves are allowed. Not knowing the building or the people on day one is normal. You will be fine.
    6. Dining alone is fine. Sometimes necessary. Bring yourself. Sit. Eat.
    7. Backpack basics. Laptop, headphones, notebook, pens. Dress for cold classrooms even when it is hot outside.
    8. Big halls and small ones. Lecture overwhelm happens. Hard classes still have help ... talk to the professor and use support.
    9. Get ahead when you can. Pre-read if the syllabus asks. Finish tomorrow’s piece when you have energy.
    10. Meet people gently. Class chats before move-in, sit next to someone, say hi in the dorm. Invite chains help. Friendships show themselves over time.
    11. Homesickness is real. Daily contact with family when you need it. Missing home on a birthday or firsts does not mean you chose wrong. Care packages and calls count.
    12. Overwhelm: next piece only. Heavy assignments hit. Finish the next piece. You are not the only one. Midterms and life can pile up in the same week ... ask a classmate, one day at a time.
    13. Quiet weeks count. Rest and homework are the real week. Eat before long class blocks. Early nights and stay-in Fridays are valid. Nights out are optional ... leave when you are done.
    14. Live a little offline. You do not have to document everything. Notice fall leaves and campus walks. Carry home with you while you grow into this place.
    15. Practical ops. Crimson Card, laundry, dining, room setup, parent open-when letters if you have them. Small logistics keep the week moving.
