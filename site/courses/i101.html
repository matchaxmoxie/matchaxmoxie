Introduction to informatics: Margaret Hamilton

First Luddy build: research an innovator in computing, run a small digital campaign, collect real survey data, ship a team website, and present in lab.

Innovator: Margaret Hamilton Apollo guidance software Lab MW 9:45 · WY 125

Team & rhythm

Group members: Marc Finn, Meghana Mohankumar, Jade Zhao, Anthony Coker. Standing meeting: Thursdays 4:30 p.m. at the IMU (above Starbucks). Course lead: Chase McCoy; lecture team included Logan Paul and Katie Siek (honors).

What we shipped

  • Plan & artifacts ... deep research on Hamilton; delegated roles week by week.
  • Wireframes & digital artifacts ... shared Adobe XD file for layout and campaign assets.
  • Social posts ... Instagram, Facebook, and X (Twitter) copy celebrating Hamilton’s Apollo Guidance Computer work and women in STEM.
  • Survey ... Google Forms; QR code + Reddit, Snapchat, Instagram, and group chats for distribution.
  • 118 responses ... beat our 64-response goal; cleaned in Excel (I101-Group3-Responses.xlsx).
  • Analysis & viz ... Excel and Google Data Studio on who engaged and what they learned.
  • Website + presentation ... final site submission and 5-minute lab deck; optional digital signage extra credit.

Sample campaign line (Instagram)

Meet Margaret Hamilton, the tech pioneer who engineered the Apollo Guidance Computer ... Modern software, thanks to her. #TechInnovation #MargaretHamilton #STEMChampion

What stuck

This was the first time “informatics” meant more than a definition: survey design, cleaning messy rows, coordinating async code (we used a Discord workflow for website handoff), and pitching a story in five minutes. Same muscle memory later used in I245 presentations and ServeIT client check-ins.
