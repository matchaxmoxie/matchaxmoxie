How to use Handshake

Step-by-step for any college student on Handshake (or a school job board like it). Jobs, internships, campus roles, career events ... from someone who actually uses the platform.

matchaxmoxie tip

If you’ve never used a campus job board, that’s normal. You need a working login and one honest profile line ... not a perfect plan. First-generation friendly truth: asking a coach or mentor is a skill, not a failure.

One next step is enough for today.

What Handshake does

Handshake is the campus career platform most schools use for early jobs. Employers on it are looking for students and new grads ... not five years of “senior” experience.

  • Jobs and internships ... part-time, summer, on-campus, remote, full-time later.
  • Campus / student roles ... dining, front desk, tutoring, research help, work-study style postings.
  • Career events ... fairs, info sessions, workshops.
  • Career-center appointments ... book a coach through Career Center → Appointments on many campuses.
  • Employer profiles ... research companies, follow ones you care about, see open roles.
  • Messaging ... if visibility is open, approved employers can find you. Community also unlocks peer messaging where your school supports it.
Your school’s Handshake ... search “[your university] Handshake” or use your career-center link.
IU Bloomington example: https://iubloomington.joinhandshake.com
School SSO (not a random Gmail signup). Use the campus URL your school publishes.

Events on Handshake

A lot of the career calendar lives here: fairs, employer info sessions, résumé labs, networking nights. Treat Events like a second job board.

  • Find them: open Events (sometimes under Career Center). Filter by fair, info session, workshop, or date.
  • RSVP early when seats are limited. Check virtual vs in-person and any dress or prep notes.
  • Before you go: skim the employer list, save 2 to 3 booths or sessions, and have a one-line “what I’m looking for” ready.
  • Visibility tip: some virtual fair features ask you to raise visibility from Private before you can join certain sessions.

Step by step

  1. Log in

    Use your school’s Handshake URL and campus SSO. At IU Bloomington that’s iubloomington.joinhandshake.com. Chrome or Firefox. If SSO loops, try a private window, then campus tech help.

  2. Fix visibility

    Account → Settings → Privacy. Many schools default new accounts to Private. Some (like NYU) default toward public on first login ... read the checkbox before you skip it.

    • Private ... browse and apply; employers can’t find or message you.
    • Employers ... school-approved employers can see you and reach out. Good default if you want outreach.
    • Community ... employers plus students/alumni; unlocks peer messaging where supported. No more privacy pop-ups mid-action.
    • Why it matters: CU Boulder cites ~5× more employer messages when you’re findable. Virtual fairs often require Employers or Community before you can join a session.
    • Work authorisation: answer the two questions (or opt out) in Settings so filters don’t leave you as “Unknown.”

  3. Build a profile employers can search

    Recruiters filter on experience, skills, clubs, and job interests. Complete profiles get reached out to more often. Skills are often the primary search field.

    • Education + correct grad year (auto-fill lies sometimes ... edit by hand).
    • Experience that counts: paid job, work-study, volunteer, research, family business, class project with a real deliverable, club officer. Reverse chronological. Write org names out (not acronyms). Quantify when you can.
    • Employers also search by company names you’ve worked for ... list on-campus and volunteer roles too.
    • 5 to 10 real skills (soft + technical). Prefer Handshake’s skill list so searches match.
    • Courses / projects that match roles you want (lab courses for lab jobs; class projects ... say if it was a group). Skip purely social clubs that say nothing about skills.
    • Add a portfolio, GitHub, or LinkedIn link if you have one. Photo optional. Short bio optional. Interests (job type, location, role) drive recommendations.

  4. Upload your résumé and check auto-fill

    PDF résumé → let Handshake fill fields → fix weird lines (major, grad date, GPA). One clean page beats a blank fancy template. Document visibility is separate from profile privacy: mark one résumé “visible to employers” so fair RSVP lists and coffee-chat reviews can pull it. Keep the file current.

  5. Search jobs and internships

    Jobs → filters (job type, location, industry, keyword). Save the search so new matches show up later instead of starting from zero every week. Turn on notification prefs (email / in-app) for fairs, events, and interviews. Coffee chats are sometimes posted under Jobs ... try searching “coffee chat,” not only Events.

  6. Follow employers and watch Events

    Follow 1 to 2 employers you actually care about. Check Events for fairs and info sessions. Desktop is steadier than phone for booking slots. RSVP early; if you can’t go, cancel so someone else can take the seat. When a recruiter messages you, reply ... even a short “thanks, here’s my availability.”

  7. Apply well (quality over spray)

    One posting you read beats twenty empty Quick Apply clicks. Match one résumé bullet to the role. Track what you applied to so you’re not guessing next week. Use your school email on applications.

  8. When you’re stuck → campus career center

    Career Center → Appointments (Walter Center for IU College students; other schools have their own coaches in the same menu). Use them for résumé rewrite, interview practice, or “I don’t know where to start.” Advising and scholarship holds stay with advisors ... not Handshake settings. Some schools leave Handshake for another hub (Purdue moved to Career Gateway) ... always use the link your career center publishes.
